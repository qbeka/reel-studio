What happens underneath

A video can only be watched. Because Reel keeps commands instead of pixels, every moment in the story is a working copy of the app.

Record

A scene declares commands such as setSplit. Reel stores them with their logical time. It stores no pixels, DOM changes or mouse paths.

Seek

Reel restores the nearest checkpoint and replays forward to the exact time. A newer seek replaces any seek still running.

Try it

The state on screen becomes your own copy, and the timeline forks. Nothing you do reaches the recording, the URL or storage.

Return

You pick a chapter. Reel says which ones match your copy and which clear it. The two are never merged.

How it was checked

  • An independent model of each scene, sharing no code with the app, agreed on every log. It caught 6 of 6 seeded money bugs that comparing against replay could not see.
  • Seeking matched a full replay at all 5,332 positions tested.
  • 14 of 14 seeded failures were caught. 36 of 38 mutants were killed, and the other 2 are equivalent.
  • The unit and browser suites pass on macOS and Linux. The exported viewer runs in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox.

What this cannot tell you

  • The timings come from one Apple M5 Pro laptop in headless Chromium, with other work running. They are lab numbers, not field data.
  • Reel records scenes written for it. It cannot record an arbitrary website.
  • The large logs are generated. The only real recording here has 10 commands.
  • No person has used it in a study, and the editor was tested only in Chromium.

Everything on this page is static files. The demo sends nothing and stores nothing.
